In Tanzania, livestock keeping is common in Mwanza, Mara, Shinyanga, Tabora, Singida, Dodoma, Kilimanjaro, Arusha and Manyara. Some of the ethnic groups, that practise livestock keeping are the Maasai, Barbaig, Sukuma, Nyamwezi, Chagga and Gogo. Of these, the Masai, Barbaig and Sukuma practise nomadic pastoralism. These ethnic groups move with their livestock from place to place in search of pastures and water.

Advantages of livestock keeping

There are many advantages of livestock keeping, including the provision of meat, milk, skin, hooves and manure. Animal skins are used to make various items such as shoes, bags, belts and drums. Hooves are used to make glue. Animal manure is used to fertilise soil. When animals are sold, we obtain income.

The effects of poor livestock keeping on the environment

When a large number of livestock graze in a small area, they can deplete all the grass and other vegetation, leaving the land bare, as illustrated in Figure 8. Likewise, when large groups of livestock walk over the same area for a long time, they remove vegetation cover and loosen the soils. When the soil is loose, it is easily eroded by wind or water. Furthermore, nomadic pastoralism leads to the cutting down of trees for making kraals for livestock, human shelter and firewood for cooking.
