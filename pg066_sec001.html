Minimising the use of pesticides and industrial fertilisers can help to maintain soil fertility. Compost and manure can be used as a substitute of industrial fertilizers. Additionally, trees should be planted in bare lands and in places where shifting cultivation has been practised to prevent soil erosion.

Livestock keeping

Livestock keeping is an activity that involves rearing of livestock such as cows, goats, sheep, chickens and ducks. There are three types of livestock keeping: zero-grazing, free-range rearing and nomadic pastoralism. In zero-grazing, animals are kept and fed indoors. This type of grazing involves keeping a few animals. Sometimes, cattle, goats and sheep are kept on large farms owned by the government, companies or individuals. These large farms are called ranches.

Another type of livestock keeping is free-range rearing. In this kind of animal rearing, pastoralists take their livestock to grazing areas in the morning and bring them back in the evening. The other type of livestock keeping is called nomadic pastoralism. In this kind of livestock keeping, pastoralists move with their livestock from place to place, looking for pastures and water. Sometimes, livestock travel long distances before they reach a place where pastures and water are available.
